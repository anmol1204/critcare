The Liver — Structure, Dual Blood Supply & Functions

Functions of the liver (why losing it hurts everywhere)

🍬 Carbohydrate metabolism

Glycogenesis, glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis — the liver buffers blood glucose. Loss → hypoglycaemia (also ↓ insulin degradation).

🧈 Fat metabolism

Fatty-acid oxidation (→ acetoacetate), and synthesis of lipoproteins & cholesterol.

🧬 Protein metabolism

  • Amino acids → glucose / lipids
  • Synthesis of urea, plasma proteins (albumin) and clotting factors

💛 Secretory / excretory

Bile (≈ 400–800 mL/day); excretion of bilirubin, drugs & toxins.

🩸 Vascular & reticulo-endothelial

Sinusoids lined by endothelial + Kupffer cells (phagocytic); a major blood reservoir; forms hepatic lymph (portal HTN → ↑ sinusoidal pressure → ↑ lymph → ascites).

⚙️ Endocrine / metabolic

  • Metabolises IGF-1
  • Converts T4 → T3
  • Activates vitamin D
  • Metabolises drugs & hormones

Dual blood supply

Hepatic blood flow ≈ 25% of cardiac output
Total Hepatic Blood Flow~1500 mL/min · the liver is a major vascular reservoir
Hepatic artery20–30% of flowHigh O₂ content · supplies ~50% of O₂ · autoregulated
Portal vein70–80% of flowLower O₂ content but supplies ~50% of O₂ · NOT autoregulated · flow set by splanchnic bed
Delivered together to the hepatic sinusoids (perfused at low pressure)
🔑 Hepatic Arterial Buffer Response (HABR) — the exam favourite
  • The hepatic artery compensates for changes in portal flow to keep total hepatic flow (and O₂ delivery) constant. Mediated by adenosine washout:
  • ↓ Portal flow → adenosine is not washed away → accumulates around the arterioles → hepatic artery dilates (buffers the fall).
  • ↑ Portal flow → adenosine washed out → hepatic artery constricts.
  • Anaesthetic relevance: the portal vein cannot autoregulate, so hepatic O₂ delivery depends heavily on the HABR — and the HABR is impaired by anaesthesia, hypotension, hypocapnia and surgical traction. Volatiles (esp. halothane) and low CO reduce hepatic flow → the diseased liver becomes ischaemic. Isoflurane best preserves hepatic flow among the older volatiles.
Regulation of hepatic blood flow (HBF)
Intrinsic: HABR (adenosine) + pressure–flow autoregulation (hepatic artery only) + transmural pressure (↑ → vasoconstriction).
Extrinsic: Neural — sympathetic stimulation → α-mediated portal venous & arterial vasoconstriction; Humoral — glucagon, secretin (vasodilate), catecholamines, angiotensin, vasopressin (vasoconstrict).
🧪 Liver function tests — read them like an anaesthetist
  • Hepatocellular injury: ALT, AST.
  • Cholestasis: ALP, 5′-nucleotidase, GGT.
  • Detoxification / excretory: total & direct bilirubin, serum ammonia.
  • Synthetic function: serum albumin, globulin, and the coagulation profile / PT.
  • PT/INR is the single best acute marker of synthetic function — clotting-factor half-lives are short (~1–12 h) so PT rises quickly, whereas albumin (t½ 18–20 days) reflects chronic synthetic capacity.
  • All coagulation factors are made in the liver except von Willebrand factor and factor VIII (endothelium / megakaryocytes) — hence factor VIII can be normal or high even in advanced disease. Vitamin-K-dependent factors = II, VII, IX, X and proteins C, S, Z.
