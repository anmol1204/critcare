🛏️ 2 · Recovery

Reversal & Extubation

🫁 Check the airway before you extubate
  • Ensure adequate reversal of neuromuscular block (deep block was needed for immobility) — TOF-guided.
  • Before extubation, rule out airway/laryngeal oedema (consider a cuff-leak test) after prolonged steep Trendelenburg.
  • Some patients need post-operative ventilation — e.g. a history of hypercarbia, significant laryngeal oedema, or delayed emergence from cerebral oedema.
  • Keep blood products available; monitor for delayed bleeding.
⚠️ 3 · Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Mistake 1

Inadequate depth/relaxation with the robot docked → patient movement → visceral injury.

Mistake 2

Not re-checking the ETT after steep Trendelenburg + insufflation → unrecognised endobronchial intubation.

Mistake 3

Liberal fluids early → airway/facial oedema, obscured field and POVL risk.

Mistake 4

Extubating without checking for airway oedema after a long, steep case.

Mistake 5

Poor eye protection / padding — corneal abrasion, brachial-plexus injury — irreversible once docked.

🎓 4 · Exam Pearls

Viva-Ready Pearls

Steep Trendelenburg + pneumoperitoneum → ↑ preload, ↑ CO, ↑ SVR → ↑ myocardial O₂ demand; ↓ compliance; ↑ ICP/IOP.

POVL = ischaemic optic neuropathy from prolonged ↑ IOP/venous congestion — a signature robotic-prostatectomy complication.

Restrictive fluids until the urethral anastomosis is complete; give fluid afterwards.

Total immobility is mandatory — the patient cannot move while the arms are docked.

Relative contraindications to steep Trendelenburg: raised ICP, glaucoma, severe cardiac/respiratory disease, morbid obesity.
