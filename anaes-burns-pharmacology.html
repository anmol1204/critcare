☠️ 2 · Suxamethonium

Suxamethonium — the Lethal Hyperkalaemia

Why suxamethonium kills the healing burns patient
Burn injury → up-regulation & spread of extrajunctional (immature, fetal-type) ACh receptorsReceptors proliferate across the whole muscle membrane, not just the motor end-plate
Suxamethonium depolarises this vast receptor fieldMassive efflux of K⁺ from muscle into the circulation
Acute severe hyperkalaemia → peaked T-waves → VT/VF / cardiac arrest
The rule (Dr. Tanya's note)

Avoid suxamethonium after ~24–48 hours from the burn, up to ~1–2 years (until the burn is fully healed and the receptor population normalises). In the first 24 hours the receptor up-regulation has not yet developed, so suxamethonium is still considered safe if truly needed for a rapid-sequence induction. The safest habit for the healing/contracted patient is a rocuronium modified-RSI instead.

💉 3 · Non-Depolarisers

Resistance to Non-Depolarising Relaxants

🔼 Bigger doses, shorter effect
  • The same receptor up-regulation that makes suxamethonium dangerous makes the patient resistant to non-depolarising relaxants — more receptors must be occupied to achieve block.
  • Add altered pharmacokinetics: hypermetabolism → ↑ clearance; a larger volume of distribution.
  • Result: dose requirements may be substantially increased and duration shortened — titrate to neuromuscular (TOF) monitoring rather than to a weight-based dose.
⚗️ 4 · Altered Drug Handling

The Hypermetabolic Patient & Drug Binding

Protein binding shifts

  • ↓ Albumin (protein loss/catabolism) → ↑ free fraction of acidic, albumin-bound drugs (e.g. diazepam, phenytoin) → exaggerated effect
  • ↑ α₁-acid glycoprotein → ↑ binding of basic drugs (e.g. some local anaesthetics, opioids) → ↓ free fraction

Practical consequences

  • High opioid requirement & tolerance from chronic pain/repeated exposure — plan multimodal analgesia
  • Hyperdynamic circulation → faster onset of IV agents but ↑ clearance
  • Ketamine is a useful, cardiostable, analgesic induction/maintenance agent in these patients
