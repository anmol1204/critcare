Altered Drug Handling in Liver Disease

"Hepatic dysfunction changes both what dose is needed and how long a drug lasts. A large volume of distribution and reduced protein binding may demand a normal or larger initial dose, while impaired metabolism and reduced hepatic blood flow prolong the effect. Titrate to effect, choose drugs with organ-independent clearance, and expect a slower recovery."

Synthesised from Stoelting's Pharmacology & Physiology in Anesthetic Practice & Miller's Anesthesia.

Three pharmacokinetic changes

📉 What changes and why
  • ① Large volume of distribution (ascites, ↑ total body water) → the initial dose may need to be larger — but because of hepatocellular dysfunction & reduced metabolism the effect is prolonged.
  • ② ↓ Albumin → ↑ free (unbound) drug fraction → a smaller dose gives the same clinical effect for highly protein-bound drugs (e.g. thiopentone) — reduce and titrate.
  • ③ ↓ Rate of metabolism & ↓ hepatic blood flow → prolonged duration of action.
  • Overarching goal in theatre: maintain hepatic blood flow & oxygenation — maintain cardiac output (which maintains hepatic & renal flow), avoid hypotension, hypocapnia and high airway pressures.

Hepatic metabolism & the extraction ratio

High extraction ratio (>0.7)Low extraction ratio (<0.4)
Rate-limiting stepFlow-limited — clearance depends on hepatic blood flowCapacity-limited — clearance depends on enzyme capacity & protein binding
Vulnerable to↓ Hepatic blood flow (hypotension, low CO, volatiles)Enzyme dysfunction / induction / hypoalbuminaemia
ExamplesPropofol, lignocaine, morphine, pethidine, fentanylThiopentone, diazepam, phenytoin, warfarin, rifampicin

Phase I reactions (oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis — CYP450) are affected earlier than Phase II (conjugation, glucuronidation, sulfation, acetylation, methylation), which is relatively preserved — hence drugs cleared by conjugation (e.g. lorazepam, oxazepam) are "safer" than those needing oxidation.

⭐ The two drugs to remember for the "organ-independent" answer
  • Neuromuscular blocker of choice = atracurium / cisatracurium (Hofmann elimination — no reliance on liver or kidney).
  • Opioid of choice = remifentanil (plasma esterase metabolism); fentanyl is the best bolus opioid. Premedication should be minimal — avoid sedatives in encephalopathy (a small dose of midazolam is acceptable in early/mild disease).
