💥 Anaesthesia for the Burns Patient

"The burns patient challenges the anaesthetist twice: acutely, as a shocked patient with an airway that is closing; and later, as a hypermetabolic, contracted patient for repeated excision and grafting. The two phases demand different plans — but both punish hypothermia, under-resuscitation and a casual airway."

How to use this topic — CritCare.in.
📘 1 · The Problem

Anaesthesia for the Burns Patient

🎯 Two very different presentations
  • The acute burn (hours–days) — a resuscitation problem: a threatened airway (thermal/inhalation injury), massive capillary leak & hypovolaemia, carbon-monoxide/cyanide toxicity and the need for escharotomy. This is the "ebb" (hypovolaemic) phase.
  • The healing / contracted burn (weeks–months) — a hypermetabolic patient presenting for repeated excision, split-skin grafting and contracture release, with a difficult airway from neck/face contractures, altered drug handling and major intra-operative blood & heat loss. This is the "flow" (hypermetabolic) phase.
🧠 The one-line summary
  • Secure the airway early; resuscitate to a urine output, not a formula; from ~24–48 h to ~1–2 years avoid suxamethonium (lethal hyperkalaemia) and expect resistance to non-depolarisers; and treat the burns patient as one who will lose heat, blood and protein faster than anyone else on your list.
