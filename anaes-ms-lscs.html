📘 1 · Stoelting / Chestnut — The Problem

Mitral Stenosis in Pregnancy for Caesarean Section

"Mitral stenosis is the valve lesion that pregnancy punishes most. A fixed, narrowed valve meets a 50% rise in blood volume, a rise in heart rate and a fall in systemic vascular resistance — the very changes a stenotic mitral valve cannot accommodate — and the parturient tips into pulmonary oedema. The anaesthetic goal is a slow, full, well-controlled circulation."

Synthesised from Stoelting's Co-Existing Disease & Chestnut's Obstetric Anesthesia.
🎯 Illustrative case

A 24-year-old primigravida at ~35 weeks, a known case of mitral stenosis, planned for elective lower-segment caesarean section (LSCS) (a classic DrNB long case — June 2019 Paper 3). The principles below apply to any parturient with significant MS.

🧠 The one-line summary
  • MS is a fixed obstruction to left-ventricular filling. Anything that shortens diastole (tachycardia), drops preload, or raises pulmonary vascular resistance is dangerous. Aim to keep the patient slow, in sinus rhythm, adequately filled, and free of rises in PVR.
