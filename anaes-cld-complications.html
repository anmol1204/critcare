Ascites, Varices & Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis

Ascites — pathogenesis

Why fluid accumulates
Sinusoidal portal hypertension
↑ Hydrostatic pressure in hepatic sinusoids
Hypoalbuminaemia → ↓ oncotic pressure
Leakage of fluid + hepatic lymph into the peritoneal cavity
+ splanchnic hypervolaemia / central hypovolaemia → RAAS → Na⁺ & H₂O retention
Ascites
🧫 SAAG, grades & treatment
  • SAAG (serum-ascites albumin gradient = serum − ascites albumin): ≥1.1 g/dL → portal hypertension (cirrhosis, alcoholic hepatitis, cardiac); <1.1 → non-portal causes (peritoneal TB/malignancy, nephrotic, pancreatic).
  • Grades: I — detected only on USG (<500 mL, puddle sign +); II — moderate (500 mL–2 L, shifting dullness +); III — tense (>2 L, fluid thrill +).
  • Rx: ① salt-restricted diet; ② diuretics — spironolactone : furosemide in a 100 : 40 mg ratio (up to 400 : 160 mg/day); ③ therapeutic paracentesis.
  • Refractory ascites = no response despite maximal diuretics. For large-volume paracentesis (>5 L) give albumin ~8 g per litre removed to prevent post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction.

Management of acute variceal bleeding Emergency
① Resuscitate — restrictive transfusion (target Hb ~7–8 g/dL), protect the airway (high aspiration risk — RSI).
② Control bleeding — pharmacological: terlipressin 2 mg IV 6-hourly → 1 mg 6-hourly until bleeding stops (or vasopressin 20 U in 100 mL D5 over 15 min then 0.1–0.4 U/min); or octreotide (somatostatin analogue → splanchnic vasoconstriction). Add antibiotic prophylaxis.
③ Endoscopy (urgent): variceal band ligation or sclerotherapy (sodium tetradecyl sulphate).
④ Balloon tamponade (rescue): Sengstaken–Blakemore (3-lumen) / Minnesota (4-lumen).
⑤ Salvage: TIPS (transjugular intrahepatic porto-systemic shunt, portal → hepatic vein) — but precipitates encephalopathy.
Prophylaxis: non-selective β-blockers (propranolol, nadolol) — block β in the gut → unopposed α → splanchnic vasoconstriction → ↓ portal pressure.

Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP)

Infection of ascitic fluid without an intra-abdominal source. Diagnose on ascitic PMN >250 cells/mm³ (typically a single organism; ascitic protein often low). Rx: 3rd-generation cephalosporin + IV albumin (reduces HRS); secondary prophylaxis with a quinolone. A perioperative decompensation trigger — always exclude it in the acutely unwell cirrhotic.
