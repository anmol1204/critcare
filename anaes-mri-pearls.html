🏷️ 2 · Terminology

MR-Safety Labelling

LabelMeaning
MR Safe (green square)Poses no known hazard in any MR environment — non-conducting, non-metallic, non-magnetic
MR Conditional (yellow triangle)Safe under specified conditions (field strength, gradient, RF limits) — most anaesthetic equipment used in-room is this
MR Unsafe (red circle)Ferromagnetic / hazardous — must never enter the MR environment
⚠️ 3 · Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Mistake 1

Taking unchecked / ferromagnetic equipment (steel O₂ cylinder, laryngoscope, standard pump) into Zone IV → projectile.

Mistake 2

Looped/coiled cables against skin → RF burns.

Mistake 3

Trying to resuscitate inside the magnet room instead of evacuating the patient first.

Mistake 4

Lowering the standard of monitoring/assistance because it is "just a scan" — remote-location anaesthesia demands full theatre standards.

🎓 4 · Exam Pearls

Viva-Ready Pearls

The static field is always on — you cannot switch the magnet off for an emergency (only a quench).

Four ACR zones: I public → II screening → III control room → IV magnet.

The projectile effect is the signature lethal hazard; ECG is distorted by the magnetohydrodynamic effect.

Golden rule: in an emergency, get the patient out of Zone IV and resuscitate outside the field.

Gadolinium → NSF in severe renal impairment; scan noise → hearing protection.
