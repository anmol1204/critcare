"MRI is the hardest 'easy' anaesthetic: a still, cooperative patient in a dim tunnel far from help, in a room where a steel laryngoscope becomes a bullet and the magnet never switches off. Safety here is about the environment as much as the patient — and the golden rule when things go wrong is to get the patient out of the magnet room."

How to use this topic — CritCare.in.
📘 1 · The Problem

Anaesthesia in the MRI Suite

🎯 A remote (non-operating-room) location with unique physics

MRI is a form of remote-location / non-operating-room anaesthesia (NORA): an unfamiliar, isolated environment with limited access to the patient during scanning — plus a powerful, always-on static magnetic field, switching gradient fields and radiofrequency pulses that create hazards found nowhere else in the hospital.

🧠 The one-line summary
  • Respect the safety zones and the projectile hazard; use MR-conditional equipment & monitoring; deliver a NORA anaesthetic to the full standard you would in theatre; and if the patient deteriorates, evacuate them out of the magnet room before resuscitating.
