Anaesthetic Options for LSCS

Vaginal delivery is recommended whenever possible (with a good regional block and an assisted second stage); caesarean is for obstetric indications or decompensated disease. The choice below is for when LSCS is planned.

🟢 Graded epidural (preferred)

  • Slow, titrated epidural — the total dose is titrated to the desired level, so the fall in SVR/preload is gradual and controllable.
  • Best tolerated in NYHA I–II (mild–moderate) MS; slower onset is an advantage here.

🔴 Single-shot spinal (avoided)

  • A single-shot spinal is avoided — the sudden fall in SVR & preload and the reflex tachycardia are poorly tolerated by a fixed valve.

🔵 General anaesthesia

  • For NYHA III–IV, decompensated disease, or when regional is contraindicated.
  • Attenuate the laryngoscopy/intubation pressor response — opioids (remifentanil/fentanyl) ± β-blocker; modified RSI using etomidate + remifentanil + suxamethonium; avoid tachycardia and light planes; ensure adequate depth.
  • Disadvantages of GA: haemodynamic swings, positive-pressure ventilation worsening a high PVR/PAH. Advantages: airway control, TEE available, opioid-based technique is cardiostable with excellent analgesia; avoid large doses of volatile; avoid N₂O in severe PAH (use 50:50 O₂:N₂O only if PAH mild).
