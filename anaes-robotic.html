"In robotic surgery the surgeon sits at a console while a bulky robot is docked to an immobile patient in steep head-down tilt with a CO₂-filled abdomen. The anaesthetist trades easy access to the patient for a set of predictable physiological insults — and must keep the patient perfectly still, because the patient cannot move once the arms are locked."

📘 1 · The Problem

Anaesthesia for Robot-Assisted Surgery

🎯 The archetype — robot-assisted radical prostatectomy (RARP)

A newer field of surgery in which operations are carried out with the surgeon at a robotic console while the robot is docked at the surgical site. The pelvic prostatectomy is the classic case — done in steep Trendelenburg with CO₂ pneumoperitoneum — and the same principles extend to robotic gynae-oncology, colorectal and renal surgery.

🧠 The one-line summary
  • Anticipate the physiology of steep Trendelenburg + pneumoperitoneum (↑ myocardial demand, ↓ compliance, ↑ ICP/IOP), select patients who can tolerate it, and manage the docked robot — total immobility, restricted patient & airway access, meticulous positioning/padding and eye protection — with restrictive fluids until the anastomosis is done.
