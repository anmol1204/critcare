🛏️ 2 · Emergence

Reversal & Extubation

Deep extubation

  • Often preferred — avoids the bronchospasm of extubating a lightly-anaesthetised, irritable airway
  • Smooth reversal; bridge with NIV; inhaled bronchodilator or IV lignocaine at extubation
  • Caveat: neostigmine itself may provoke bronchospasm; risk of aspiration/obstruction/hypoventilation

Awake extubation instead if…

  • Severe COPD, chronic hypoxaemia & CO₂ retention
  • Full-stomach / aspiration risk, difficult airway
  • Ensure complete neuromuscular reversal — residual block is a major PPC risk factor
🫁 3 · PPCs

Post-op Pulmonary Complications & Prevention

The major PPCs
  • Atelectasis · retained secretions · hypercapnic respiratory failure · need for ventilatory support · pneumonia.
PhaseMeasures to reduce PPCs
Pre-opSmoking cessation ≥ 6–8 weeks; treat airflow obstruction & infection; educate on lung-expansion manoeuvres
Intra-opMinimally-invasive surgery when possible; consider regional; lung-protective ventilation
Post-opLung-expansion (incentive spirometry, chest physio), maximise analgesia (multimodal, cautious opioids), early mobilisation, warm & well-oxygenated with PaCO₂ near the pre-op baseline, NIV in high-risk patients, continue bronchodilators/steroids
🎓 4 · Take-Home & Pearls

Take-Home Messages

COPD + laparoscopy = a double hit on respiratory mechanics.

The most dangerous intra-op issue is dynamic hyperinflation (auto-PEEP) — always prioritise a long expiratory time.

Accept permissive hypercapnia (pH > 7.25); don't chase a normal CO₂ by hyperventilating.

Post-operative care determines outcome as much as intra-operative management.

Diagnosis needs post-bronchodilator FEV₁/FVC < 0.7; SI = cigs/day × years; pack-years = (cigs/day × years)/20.
