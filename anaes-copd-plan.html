⚖️ 2 · RA vs GA

Regional versus General Anaesthesia

The principle
  • Regional anaesthesia, wherever feasible, is preferred — it eliminates airway manipulation, avoids the pressor response and polypharmacy, gives profound analgesia and reduces DVT & blood loss. GA carries increased respiratory morbidity in COPD.

Regional — advantages

  • No airway manipulation; avoids pressor response
  • Profound analgesia; avoids polypharmacy & systemic opioids
  • Less blood loss, ↓ DVT, faster ambulation & recovery

Regional — cautions

  • A sensory block above T6 impairs the accessory/abdominal muscles → ↓ ERV, PEFR & MVV
  • Inadequate cough → retained secretions
  • Titrate the level; high blocks are poorly tolerated
💊 3 · Premedication & Agents

Premedication & Drug Selection for GA

Premedication

  • Cautious sedatives — blunted hypoxic/hypercapnic drive; but anxiolysis helps (anxiety → hyperventilation, CO₂ wash-out, dynamic hyperinflation)
  • Avoid long-acting benzodiazepines & excess opioids
  • Continue bronchodilators (nebs/inhalers), steroids & nicotine-replacement; anticholinergic to dry secretions
  • Optimise pre-oxygenation — CPAP may help in severe disease

Induction & maintenance

  • Consider supraglottic device over ETT — minimise airway manipulation
  • Induction: propofol or ketamine; avoid morphine & barbiturates (may provoke bronchospasm)
  • Blunt airway reflexes: IV/laryngotracheal lignocaine, fentanyl
  • Maintenance: sevoflurane (bronchodilator); avoid desflurane (airway irritant) and N₂O (expands bullae)
  • Relaxants: non-histamine-releasing (vecuronium, rocuronium); ensure full reversal
🎈 4 · Laparoscopy

The Laparoscopy Problem — Pneumoperitoneum & Trendelenburg

Why laparoscopic surgery is a "double hit" in COPD
Pneumoperitoneum (CO₂)↑ intra-abdominal pressure → ↓ FRC & ↓ compliance; CO₂ absorption → hypercarbia → may need ↑ minute ventilation, which COPD tolerates poorly → risk of respiratory acidosis
Trendelenburg positionFurther ↓ lung volumes, ↑ airway pressures, ↑ atelectasis

Practical response: keep insufflation pressure as low as the surgeon allows, watch EtCO₂ and airway pressures, do serial ABGs in prolonged cases, accept permissive hypercapnia, and optimise the ventilator strategy (next tab).
