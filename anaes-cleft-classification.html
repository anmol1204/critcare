Embryology & Classification

Embryology: the lip & primary palate form by fusion of the medial nasal and maxillary processes at ~5–6 weeks; the secondary palate forms by fusion of the palatal shelves at ~8–12 weeks. Failure of fusion → cleft. The incisive foramen divides primary (anterior) from secondary (posterior) palate. Cleft lip ± palate is more common in males; isolated cleft palate is more common in females. Cleft lip may be unilateral (commoner on the left) or bilateral.

Veau classification of cleft palate (1931)Description
Group I (A)Defect of the soft palate only
Group II (B)Hard + soft palate, extending no further than the incisive foramen
Group III (C)Complete unilateral cleft — soft palate to alveolus, usually involving the lip
Group IV (D)Complete bilateral cleft — pre-maxilla suspended from the nasal septum

Note: the Veau system does not include isolated cleft lip. The descriptive LAHSHAL notation (Lip–Alveolus–Hard palate–Soft palate, right→left) is also used to record exactly which structures are cleft.
