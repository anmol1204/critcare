🧬 2 · Background

Embryology & Classification

🧬 Embryology in one screen
  • Upper lip & primary palate — at the 4th–6th week five facial swellings form around the stomodeum (one frontonasal prominence, paired maxillary and mandibular prominences). The two medial nasal processes fuse in the midline → central lip, columella, nasal tip and intermaxilla / primary palate; they fuse with the maxillary prominences laterally → the rest of the upper lip. Failure here → cleft lip ± primary palate.
  • Secondary palate — at the 6th week the palatal shelves grow vertically down from the maxillary prominences, lateral to the tongue; at 7–8 weeks the tongue descends, the shelves rotate to horizontal and fuse with each other, with the primary palate and with the nasal septum — complete by ~10 weeks. Failure here → cleft of the secondary palate.
  • The incisive foramen is the landmark dividing the primary (anterior) from the secondary (posterior) palate.
  • Cleft lip ± palate is commoner in males (and, when unilateral, on the left); isolated cleft palate is commoner in females.
🔖 Descriptive types

Types of cleft lip & cleft palate

Cleft Lip

  • Unilateral, bilateral or median
  • Complete — full width of lip + alveolus, extending up to the floor of the nose
  • Incomplete — part of the lip, not reaching the nasal floor
  • Microform (forme fruste) — a notch in the vermilion border only

Cleft Palate

  • May involve primary, secondary, or both; unilateral, bilateral or median
  • Complete — primary + secondary palate, including soft palate and uvula
  • Incomplete — cleft of part of the palate
  • Submucous — muscle cleft covered by intact mucosa (bifid uvula, zona pellucida, notched hard palate) — not obvious on inspection
🏛️ Veau (1931)

Veau classification of the cleft palate

Veau groupDescription
Group I (A)Defect of the soft palate only
Group II (B)Hard + soft palate, extending no further than the incisive foramen
Group III (C)Complete unilateral cleft — soft palate to alveolus, usually involving the lip
Group IV (D)Complete bilateral cleft (resembles Group III but bilateral) — the pre-maxilla is suspended from the nasal septum

Limitation: the Veau system does not include the isolated cleft lip.

🔤 LAHSHAL

LAHSHAL / LAHSAL descriptive code

A striped-Y notation that records exactly which structures are cleft. The mouth is divided and read as if looking at the patient — the first character is the patient's right, the last the patient's left:

LAHSHAL
Lip (R)Alveolus (R)Hard palate (R)Soft palateHard palate (L)Alveolus (L)Lip (L)

A capital letter = complete cleft of that structure, a lower-case letter = incomplete cleft, and a dot (·) = no cleft. (The 6-structure LAHSAL variant merges the hard palate into a single character.)
