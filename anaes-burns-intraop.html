🎯 2 · Intra-op Concerns

The Intra-operative Checklist (from Dr. Tanya's notes)

🔑 Eight recurring concerns for burns surgery
  • Difficult monitoring — attaching leads to burnt/grafted skin
  • Difficult airway & mask ventilation — cart ready, inhalational induction preferred
  • Hypothermia — the dominant intra-op threat
  • Prolonged procedure & multiple surgeries → more anaesthetic agent/analgesia
  • Special positioning for donor-skin harvest
  • Muscle relaxant choice — avoid suxamethonium, NDMR resistance
  • Adrenaline-soaked swabs → tachycardia/arrhythmia
  • Haemodynamic support & fluid/blood management — major blood loss
🌡️ 3 · Hypothermia

Preventing Hypothermia — the Priority

Burns patients lose heat faster than any other surgical patient: a large exposed raw surface, loss of the skin's insulating barrier, huge evaporative & radiant losses, and impaired hypothalamic thermoregulation. Hypothermia worsens coagulopathy, slows drug metabolism and delays reversal.

Aim

  • Keep the patient warm — limit heat loss to ≤~1 °C from baseline
  • Post as the first case of the day

How

  • Warm the theatre to ~28–32 °C
  • Forced-air warming & warming mattress; cover unburnt areas
  • Fluid & blood warmers; humidified warm gases
  • Minimise exposure time; warm skin-prep solutions
🩸 4 · Blood Loss

Blood Loss & Adrenaline

💉 Major, rapid, hard to measure
  • Excision & grafting bleeds briskly from the raw dermis — blood loss is easily underestimated (soaked into drapes/swabs) and can exceed 30 mL/kg.
  • Reserve & cross-match blood products pre-operatively; calculate the maximum allowable blood loss (MABL) and transfusion trigger in advance.
  • Surgeons use adrenaline-soaked swabs / tumescent adrenaline to reduce bleeding — this can be systemically absorbed → tachycardia and arrhythmias (especially with volatile agents). Communicate and monitor.
  • Have vasopressors/inotropes ready — hypotension is common in the event of blood loss.
📟 5 · Monitoring

Monitoring the Burnt Patient

MonitorThe burns-specific problem
SpO₂Unreliable in CO poisoning (reads COHb as O₂Hb); probe may not fit burnt digits — try ear/nose
ECGGel electrodes won't stick to burnt/grafted skin — use skin-staple / subcutaneous needle electrodes clipped with crocodile clips
NIBP / IBPNIBP cuff may not fit; for prolonged cases with large blood loss use invasive arterial (IBP) monitoring
TemperatureCore temperature — essential given the hypothermia risk
Urine outputResuscitation endpoint (0.5–1 mL/kg/h)
EtCO₂ & TOFVentilation; neuromuscular monitoring (dose titration in NDMR resistance)

Positioning: plan around the location of burns and the donor-skin sites (often thighs/back) — special positioning & careful padding are frequently needed, and the "burnt" and "donor" areas are both surgical fields.
