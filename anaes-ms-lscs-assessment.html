Clinical Assessment & Severity Grading

History — symptoms of decompensation
Dyspnoea & orthopnoea (pulmonary congestion), haemoptysis, easy fatigability and syncope (fixed low output), palpitations (AF), symptoms of right heart failure (swelling, abdominal distension).
🩺 Examination signs
  • Typical mitral facies (malar flush) · mild peripheral cyanosis.
  • Loud S₁ (a pliable, non-calcified valve snaps shut from a high LA pressure; a soft S₁ suggests a calcified valve).
  • Opening snap (OS) — a shorter S₂–OS interval means more severe stenosis (higher LA pressure opens the valve sooner).
  • Low-pitched, rumbling mid-diastolic murmur with presystolic accentuation (if in sinus rhythm — reflects preserved atrial kick).
  • Loud P₂ and a right parasternal heave (pulmonary hypertension & RV hypertrophy); signs of a tricuspid murmur / raised JVP with RV failure.
InvestigationFindings
ECGP-mitrale (broad, bifid P in II) = LA enlargement; AF; RVH (dominant R in V₁–V₂)
EchocardiographyValve area (planimetry / pressure-half-time), reduced anterior-leaflet motion, LA enlargement/thrombus, PAP, thickened valve, diastolic doming
Chest X-ray↑ Cardiothoracic ratio, straightening of the left heart border & double density (LA enlargement), elevated left main bronchus, splaying of the carina, upper-lobe diversion
