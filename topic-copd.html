🫁 1 · Overview & Definition

Acute exacerbation of COPD (AECOPD)

"An exacerbation is an acute worsening of dyspnoea, cough and/or sputum beyond normal day-to-day variation. Every precipitant does the same thing — narrows airways, worsens airflow limitation and drives dynamic hyperinflation, which in turn produces the hypoxaemia and hypercapnia that bring the patient to the ICU."

Summarised from Irwin & Rippe's Intensive Care Medicine; with Marino's The ICU Book, Oh's Intensive Care Manual and GOLD.

COPD is defined by persistent airflow limitation (post-bronchodilator FEV₁/FVC <0.7). The ICU sees the severe end: respiratory failure that is a mix of lung failure (V/Q mismatch, shunt) and pump failure (fatigued muscles working against hyperinflation).

🎯 2 · Precipitants

What triggers an exacerbation

TriggerNote
Respiratory infection (viral/bacterial)The commonest cause; drives sputum change
Air pollution / environmentalLinked to symptoms and mortality
Non-adherence / undertreatmentMissed inhalers, poor technique
Comorbid triggersHeart failure, PE, pneumothorax, arrhythmia — always exclude
~30% unknownA subgroup is eosinophilic (steroid-responsive)

"Exacerbations beget exacerbations." A previous exacerbation is the strongest predictor of the next — and always exclude the mimics (PE, pneumothorax, decompensated heart failure) before blaming COPD.

🔬 3 · Pathophysiology

Dynamic hyperinflation & auto-PEEP

1

Airflow limitation worsens

Bronchospasm, mucosal oedema and secretions narrow already-diseased small airways; expiratory flow falls.

2

Incomplete exhalation → gas trapping

The next breath starts before the last is exhaled → dynamic hyperinflation and auto-PEEP (intrinsic PEEP).

3

Mechanical & haemodynamic penalty

Flattened diaphragm at a mechanical disadvantage → ↑ work of breathing; raised intrathoracic pressure → ↓ venous return → hypotension.

4

Gas-exchange failure

V/Q mismatch → hypoxaemia; muscle fatigue + increased dead space → hypercapnia & respiratory acidosis (Type 2 failure).

🫁 6 · Respiratory Support

From NIV to the ventilator

"Non-invasive ventilation is the single intervention that most changes outcome in the acidotic hypercapnic exacerbation — it lowers intubation, length of stay and mortality. Invasive ventilation is a rescue, and its whole art is giving enough minute ventilation while avoiding gas trapping."

Summarised from Irwin & Rippe; Oh's Intensive Care Manual; ERS/ATS.
ModalityWhenKey point
NIV (BiPAP)First line for respiratory acidosis (pH <7.35 with hypercapnia) despite initial therapyIPAP to offload work & clear CO₂; EPAP to counter auto-PEEP; reassess in 1–2 h
HFNOHypoxaemia, NIV intolerance/breaksComfort, secretion clearance, some CO₂ washout
Invasive ventilationNIV failure/contraindication, ↓ consciousness, arrest, exhaustionSee strategy below
Invasive ventilation strategy — beat the gas trapping
  • Prolong expiration: low rate (10–14), high inspiratory flow, short Ti, I:E 1:3–1:4.
  • Modest tidal volume (~6–8 mL/kg PBW); accept permissive hypercapnia.
  • Watch auto-PEEP (expiratory hold); set applied PEEP below intrinsic PEEP to ease triggering.
  • Sudden hypotension → suspect dynamic hyperinflation → disconnect and let the chest deflate (exclude pneumothorax).
🎯 7 · Weaning & Prevention

Getting off, and staying off

  • Weaning: daily spontaneous breathing trials; extubate to NIV in hypercapnic patients to prevent re-intubation; consider tracheostomy if prolonged.
  • Prevention bundle: smoking cessation, inhaler optimisation & technique, vaccination, pulmonary rehabilitation, long-term O₂ if chronically hypoxaemic, and an exacerbation action plan.
🚫 8 · Common Mistakes

Common mistakes

❌ 1 — Uncontrolled high-flow oxygen

Driving SpO₂ to 100% worsens hypercapnia and can cause narcosis. Target 88–92% with controlled devices.

❌ 2 — Delaying NIV in acidotic hypercapnia

NIV works best started early for pH <7.35 — waiting costs intubations and lives.

❌ 3 — Fast ventilator rates

Short expiration → auto-PEEP → hypotension. Use low rates and long expiratory times.

❌ 4 — Missing the mimic

PE, pneumothorax and heart failure masquerade as "just COPD" — actively exclude them.

❌ 5 — Sedating the tiring patient

Sedatives blunt respiratory drive in the unsupported hypercapnic patient — support the airway instead.

🎓 9 · Exam Pearls — DrNB / IDCCM / IFCCM

Exam pearls

Q: Oxygen target in AECOPD and why?
SpO₂ 88–92%. Hyperoxia worsens hypercapnia through V/Q redistribution, the Haldane effect and reduced hypoxic drive.

Q: When is NIV indicated?
Respiratory acidosis (pH <7.35) with hypercapnia persisting after initial medical therapy — first-line, reduces intubation and mortality.

Q: Steroid regimen?
Prednisolone 40 mg/day for 5 days (REDUCE) shortens recovery without benefit from longer courses.

Q: Ventilator strategy?
Prolong expiration (low rate, high flow, I:E 1:3–1:4), modest Vt, permissive hypercapnia, watch auto-PEEP; disconnect if it causes shock.

Q: Anthonisen criteria for antibiotics?
Increased dyspnoea, sputum volume and sputum purulence — antibiotics if ≥2 (especially purulence) or if ventilated.
