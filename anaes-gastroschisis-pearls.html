🛏️ 2 · Recovery

Post-operative Care

🫁 Usually leave intubated
  • After tight abdominal closure, compliance falls — most neonates are left intubated for elective post-operative ventilation and weaned in NICU.
  • Continue warming, glucose control, analgesia (multimodal, opioids titrated) and fluid/haemodynamic support.
  • Ongoing surveillance for abdominal compartment syndrome, sepsis and bowel ischaemia; monitor urine output, lower-limb perfusion and airway pressures.

Complications: abdominal compartment syndrome, bowel necrosis/perforation, necrotising enterocolitis, sepsis, prolonged ileus & TPN dependence, hepatic impairment, hypothermia and blood loss.

⚠️ 3 · Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Mistake 1

Using nitrous oxide — distends the bowel and defeats the reduction.

Mistake 2

Vigorous mask positive-pressure ventilation at induction → gastric/bowel distension; not decompressing the stomach first.

Mistake 3

Letting the neonate get cold — hypothermia worsens acidosis, coagulopathy and drug handling.

Mistake 4

Pushing for primary closure despite rising airway/intragastric pressures → compartment syndrome; a silo is safer.

🎓 4 · Exam Pearls

Viva-Ready Pearls

Gastroschisis = no sac (para-umbilical, right of cord); omphalocele = sac + associated anomalies.

Avoid N₂O; treat as full stomach → RSI; atracurium; keep warm; give glucose.

Stage the closure if intragastric/intravesical pressure > 20 cmH₂O, PIP > 35 cmH₂O, or EtCO₂ > 50 mmHg.

Titrate fluids to urine output 1–2 mL/kg/h; Hb goal ~10–12 g/dL.
